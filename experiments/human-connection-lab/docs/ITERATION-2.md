# Iteration 2 — from questionnaire to conversation

This records what the second iteration changed, and why. The first version is
described in [PRODUCT.md](PRODUCT.md); this document supersedes it where they
disagree.

## 1. What inspecting version one showed

The first version got one half of closeness right and missed the other.

Research on how closeness forms (the intimacy process model of Reis & Shaver)
describes two moves, not one: someone **discloses**, and the other person
**responds** in a way that shows they understood and cared. Version one made
disclosure safe and symmetric — that is the seal — but gave people no way to
respond to what was disclosed. After both halves opened, the only thing to do
was answer the next question. Two people could exchange nine deeply personal
answers and never once react to each other. That is why it felt like two
parallel monologues, or an interview.

Three further limits followed from the fixed structure:

1. **Depth on a timetable.** Three chapters of three exchanges, the same for
   every pair. A pair ready for depth after two exchanges had to wait; a pair
   never ready was pushed there anyway.
2. **A dead end.** After the ninth exchange the conversation was "complete"
   and there was nothing left to do. The product ended exactly where a
   relationship should begin.
3. **Contact exchange as the finish line.** The last reveal was an email
   address, which made leaving the product the goal, and (as the strategy
   analysis showed) mutual *consent* to share is not mutual *value*: one side
   could type nonsense and receive a real address.

## 2. Directions considered

| Direction | What it would fix | Why it was or wasn't chosen |
|---|---|---|
| Seal every message (stricter) | Nothing new | Makes the interview problem worse |
| Free chat after the first exchange | Responsiveness | Discards the seal; becomes an ordinary messenger |
| Sealed depth vote before each question | Depth on a timetable | Good, but a second mechanism beside the chapter decisions |
| **Threads + doors** (chosen) | Responsiveness, timetable, dead end, contact | One mechanism for every threshold; responsiveness where it is safe |
| Groups ("tables") first | Pair pressure | Bigger operational unknowns; worth testing later, manually |
| Public browsing feed | Discovery fun | Risks turning the product into a feed; discovery is not the weakness |

## 3. The chosen direction

**The seal guards thresholds. Between thresholds, people talk.**

- **Rounds.** A question, two sealed halves, opened together — unchanged. This
  is the first step at any depth, and it stays sealed because going first
  there is exposing.
- **Threads.** Once a round is open, either person can reply under it, and can
  pick a line from the other's half to reply to. Replies are not sealed: both
  have already disclosed at this depth, so replying is low-risk, and it is the
  responsiveness that turns disclosure into closeness. Nobody is pushed to the
  next question; either can ask for it when ready.
- **Doors.** Every threshold is a door that opens only when both have knocked:
  go a little deeper, go deep, share first names, share what we do, keep
  meeting here. A knock is private and can be taken back until the door opens.
  Nobody sees a knock that isn't answered. Doors appear when they make sense
  (names after the first round, deep only after closer), so the conversation
  still moves from light to deep — but at the pace both choose, not on a
  schedule.
- **Kept, not complete.** "Keep meeting here" replaces "complete". A kept
  conversation goes on: more rounds, more threads, and the person appears
  under *Your people* on Today, so their answer to each daily question is
  there once you've sealed yours. The relationship has a home without the
  product holding anyone hostage.

What was removed: fixed chapters, the chapter-end decision form, the "you met
halfway nine times" ending, and the in-app exchange of an email address.
Exchanging contact details is deliberately left for a later, separate and
verified step (see PRODUCT.md open questions); in this version a relationship
continues inside the product, and nothing stops people from sharing details in
a thread if they choose.

## 4. What is simulated, and how

The other people remain invented characters and the app says so.

- When you reply in a thread under their half, they reply once, with a
  pre-written continuation of their own answer. **They do not read your
  reply**; the continuation is written to follow naturally from "tell me
  more". They reply once per thread.
- They knock on doors by simple rules, and the rules reward responsiveness:
  they knock to go deeper only after you have replied to them in threads, and
  to keep meeting only after several rounds and at least one reply. Some are
  a round slower to share than others. These rules are a demo of the idea that
  depth follows responsiveness; they are not a model of real people.
- Time is still compressed: replies take seconds.

## 5. Assumptions this version makes (and must not claim to have validated)

1. Replying under a revealed answer makes the exchange feel like a
   conversation rather than an interview. (Test: hybrid vs. structured-only
   pairs, observing follow-ups and references to each other's words.)
2. Doors at one's own pace feel freer than chapter decisions, without making
   people anxious about knocks that aren't answered. (Test: interviews after a
   two-week run; watch for knock-and-undo behaviour.)
3. A kept person's answer to the daily question is a reason to come back.
   (Test: a manually run daily question among pairs who chose to keep each
   other.)

## 6. Decisions

1. Threads are unsealed; rounds and doors are sealed.
2. Either person can ask for the next question at any time; the turn to choose
   still alternates.
3. Questions are offered at the pair's shared depth; when that depth runs out,
   lighter unused questions fill in, and when everything is used the app says
   so plainly.
4. A knock can be withdrawn until the door opens. Once open, a door cannot be
   closed (what was revealed cannot be un-revealed — the app does not pretend
   otherwise).
5. The question library grew from 12 to 18 conversation questions so a kept
   relationship doesn't exhaust it immediately. It is still small; a real
   library is an editorial project (see the strategy discussion).
6. Saved worlds from version one are not migrated; the app starts fresh and
   says why.
